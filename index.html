I'm an interdisciplinary researcher exploring how people think, feel, and make decisions in AI-mediated environments.

My work sits at the intersection of Human–AI Interaction, health communication, and behavioral research. I am especially interested in what happens when AI tools communicate uncertain health information, and how their design shapes human understanding.

Before working on health-related AI experiences, I studied data science and built interactive systems. That path made me curious about a question that code alone cannot answer: how do people actually experience the technologies we create?

FIELD NOTE N° 001   /   REFLECTION

When reassurance
sounds like certainty.

In health conversations, words can be more than information. A confident tone can calm us down, but it can also make an uncertain claim feel settled.

That tension makes communication design a research problem. When does a system help someone navigate uncertainty? When does it accidentally encourage them to stop asking questions?

I want to understand that boundary — and design for the space around it.

FIELD NOTES / 00111
CLICK INTO THE MARGINS↗

Three things
worth asking.

Every study begins with an assumption. These are the ones I keep examining.

Small details — such as acknowledging someone's context — may change how attentively a response is perceived, even when its core information stays the same.

The same words can carry different expectations when attributed to a human clinician or to an AI assistant. Source cues may shape trust and accountability.

An early suggestion can remain an anchor as more information arrives. How could systems help people revise first impressions rather than merely reinforce them?
